Remote Sensing Laboratories

These laboratory activities provide hands-on experience with remote sensing data and image-processing techniques. Students work with satellite and aerial imagery while learning fundamental concepts in spectral analysis, vegetation monitoring, spatial resolution, and multi-sensor image analysis.

Laboratory 1: Comparing Satellite Resolutions for Crop Monitoring

Objective: Students compare vegetation indices from two satellites with different spatial resolutions over the same agricultural site in the border region (Sun Gro Facility, Mesilla Park, NM), and evaluate how resolution affects what can be observed.

Activities:

1. Download a Sentinel-2 L2A scene (10 m/pixel, free via Copernicus) and a PlanetScope Surface Reflectance scene (3 m/pixel) of the same site from dates as close together as possible (you will need to create a free account on the Copernicus or Planet portal).

2. Identify the Red and Near Infra Red (NIR) bands in each product, noting that each sensor stores its bands in a different order, and calculate Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) for low- and higher-spatial resolution images.

3. Produce side-by-side NDVI maps with legends and compare the mean NDVI and the level of field detail visible in each.

4. Discuss the trade-offs between free, coarser imagery and commercial, finer imagery, and how each would compare with a ground-based image collected at the same site.

Deliverables:

Side-by-side NDVI maps, a comparison table of NDVI statistics, and a short report on the trade-offs.

Tools:

Copernicus Browser, Planet Explorer, and Python (rasterio, numpy, matplotlib), with instructions that can be shared between UTEP and UACH students. Estimated time: one 3-hour session.

Laboratory 2: Crop and Soil Monitoring with NAIP Imagery

Objective: Students analyze spectrum of crops and soil and calculate vegetation indices from National Agricultural Imagery Program (NAIP) imagery (0.6 m/pixel) and compare to low spatial resolutions over the same agricultural site. Students assess how resolution affects crop and soil areas.

Activities:

1. Download a NAIP scene (0.6m /pixel, free via USGS EarthExplorer) of sites of interest around Sun Gro Facility, Mesilla Park, NM (you will need to create a free account on the USGS portal).

2. Identify all the bands available, noting that these may be different from Sentinel 2 bands. Calculate vegetation indices for these higher-spatial resolution images.

3. Produce side-by-side vegetation maps with legends and compare the mean values at the level of field detail visible in each of them.

4. Discuss the trade-offs between from NAIP and Sentinel imagery, and how each would compare with a ground-based image collected at the same site.

Deliverables:

Side-by-side vegetation maps, a comparison table of statistics, and a short report on the trade-offs.

Tools:

USGS EarthExplorer, and Python/ENVI/MATLAB, with instructions that can be shared between UTEP and UACH students. Estimated time: one 3-hour session.

Laboratory 3: Calculating Reflectance Imagery Using Hyperspectral Data Cubes

Objective: Calculate reflectance values of hyperspectral imagery, and investigate spectral properties of the vegetation and the soil.

Activities:

1. Download a hyperspectral scene (0.2 mm/pixel, free this Website). You will need to contact the webmaster to get access to the data).

2. Identify the following hyperspectral data cubes on the data repository: (a) scene cube, (b) the bright field, and (c) dark field, and download them into your computer.

3. Calculate a hyperspectral reflectance cube for each scene using the following formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

4. Inspect vegetation and soil pxels and discuss spectral similarityies and differences between them. Calculate vegetation indices and discuss similarities and differences between them.

Deliverables:

Reflectance spectra of several point accross the hyperspectral cube, and NDVI values accross the image. Write a short report on the findings.

Tools:

Scene, white, and black hypercubes. Python/ENVI/MATLAB, with instructions that can be shared between UTEP and UACH students. Estimated time: one 3-hour session.
